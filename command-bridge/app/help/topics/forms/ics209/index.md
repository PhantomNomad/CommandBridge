# ICS 209 - Incident Status Summary

The ICS 209 is a snapshot of a significant incident for people **outside** it: the agency having jurisdiction, emergency operations centres, coordination groups, elected officials and provincial or federal partners. They use it to decide what support the incident needs and how to share scarce resources when several incidents are running. It is not needed for every incident, only for ones big enough to need extra resources, draw media attention or raise the threat to the public. Your agency or the province may set when one is required.

- **Usually prepared by:** the Situation Unit Leader or other Planning Section staff.
- **Approved by:** the Planning Section Chief and/or the Incident Commander.
- **Who gets it:** the people and organizations your agency's reporting rules name (Block 15). Send an initial report, then usually one per day or per operational period, and a final one when reporting stops.

General steps for signing and printing are in [Working with ICS forms](help:forms/working_with_forms). There is also a wildfire version, the [ICS 209-WF](help:forms/ics209wf/index).

## How Command Bridge helps

- There is **one report per operational period**. The report number counts up across the incident.
- Blank fields are filled in from the previous report, then from the incident record, the assigned Incident Commander(s), your organization settings and the operational period. Blue notes show which fields were filled in this way. Nothing is kept until you save.
- A report copied forward into a new period is flagged **needs review**. Check every field; the snapshot must be current.
- Narrative boxes have **Insert from ...** buttons that offer text from the [ICS 201](help:forms/ics201/index), [ICS 202](help:forms/ics202/index), [ICS 208](help:forms/ics208/index), [ICS 213RR](help:forms/ics213rr_v2/index) and [ICS 215A](help:forms/ics215a/index). They add text; they never overwrite what you typed.
- The resource summary (Blocks 48-52) is built from the [ICS 211](help:forms/ics211/index) check-ins and the [ICS 218](help:forms/ics218/index) equipment list. Released resources are left out. You can edit every cell; press **Refresh from current resources** to rebuild it.

## What goes in the main blocks

| Blocks | What to enter |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Incident name and number. |
| 3. Report Version | Initial, Update or Final. Number the reports in order. |
| 4-5 | The Incident Commander(s) and their agency, and the kind of management organization (for example a single IC, Unified Command or an incident management team). |
| 6 | When the incident started. |
| 7-8 | How big it is (give the unit, such as hectares or city blocks) and roughly what percent is contained or done. |
| 9-10 | A short description of the incident type and its complexity level. |
| 11 | The time period this report covers. |
| 12-14 | Who prepared it, when it was sent, and who approved it. |
| 15 | Who the report is going to. |
| 16-27 | Where the incident is: province, municipality, city, jurisdiction, land ownership, coordinates and datum, a short description, and any map data attached. |
| 28 | Significant events since the last report: progress, evacuations, growth. |
| 29 | The main materials or hazards involved (chemicals, fuels, infectious agents and so on). |
| 30 | Damage: structures threatened, damaged and destroyed, by type. |
| 31-34 | People affected, both public and responders (this period and total to date), plus the life-safety threats and actions under way. Note when numbers are estimates. |
| 35 | Weather now and expected, and anything about it that causes concern. |
| 36 | How the incident is expected to behave over the next 12, 24, 48 and 72 hours and beyond. |
| 37 | Objectives: the planned end state. |
| 38 | The main threats to life, property, communities, critical infrastructure and the environment over the same time frames, and any knock-on effects. |
| 39 | Critical resources needed, in priority order, over the same time frames. |
| 40-41 | A short strategic discussion, and the main actions planned for the next period. |
| 42-46 | Expected final size, when management of the incident should finish, when large-scale demobilization should start, costs to date and the expected final cost. |
| 47 | Anything else. |
| 48-52 | The resource commitment summary (see above). |

## Submitting

To submit you must have at least the incident name, the Incident Commander(s), the incident start date, the time period (from and to) and the incident definition. Press **Submit**, then name an approver in the Sign-off box. A submitted report is locked.

Treat the 209 as a moment in time. Some of its readers may have more current information from other sources, so say where people should look for live updates if the situation is changing fast.
